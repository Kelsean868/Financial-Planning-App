# NIS Sustainability — The Actuary's Own Numbers

**Source:** *Tenth Actuarial Valuation of the National Insurance System as of 30 June 2016* — ILO report to the National Insurance Board. Scraped from nibtt.net 2026-07-16.
**Status:** The most recent published actuarial review. **This is the strongest honest argument for private retirement provision in the entire domain — and every number comes from NIS's own actuary.**

---

## ⚠️ Read this first — the discipline that must survive this finding

This material is *alarming*, which is exactly why it must be handled precisely. **The projections are conditional and dated.** The valuation is **as of 30 June 2016**, published ~2019. The 2026 reforms (rate to 16.2%, retirement age 60→65) came *after* it and **change the projections**. Anyone quoting "NIS runs out in 2035" as current fact is misusing the source.

**What is fair to say:** *the most recent published actuarial review projects X under stated assumptions, and the reforms since respond to it.* Nothing more.

---

## The headline table (10th review, base scenario)

| Milestone | Year |
|---|---|
| System's expenditure exceeds contributions | **2016–17** |
| Expenditure exceeds contributions **plus investment income** (assets start to fall) | **2023–24** |
| **Assets are exhausted** | **2035–36** |

**General Average Premium (GAP) — the contribution rate needed to fund all benefits over the projection period: 25.5%.** The legal rate at valuation was 13.2%.

> "This contribution rate is considerably higher than the current legal contribution rate of 13.2 per cent. **This conclusion is not new, it is the same as in previous actuarial valuations.** However, the magnitude and the time passed make this recommendation **more urgent today**."

---

## 🔴 The finding with real force: the recommendation was made in 2019 and implemented in 2026

> **"It is recommended to increase the contribution rate to 16.2 per cent starting in July 2019.** With such an increase, the year the projected reserve is depleted moves from 2035–36 to 2042–43. **This recommendation is however not enough to restore financial sustainability over the long term. Further contribution increases will be necessary."**

**The actuary asked for 16.2% in July 2019. It took effect 5 January 2026 — six and a half years late.**

And the actuary said plainly that 16.2% *would not be enough*. The 2027 step to 19.2% still sits **well below the 25.5% GAP** the actuary said was required.

### The full reform package the actuary recommended
The 16.2% figure was **one leg of a three-part package** — the others were **early-retirement factors** and **freezing pensions in payment for five years**. Only with all three does the table improve to:

| Milestone (with full package) | Year |
|---|---|
| Assets start to decrease | 2043–44 |
| **Assets exhausted** | **2053–54** |

**Pensions in payment were not frozen.** So the enacted reform is a partial implementation of a package the actuary presented as a whole — a materially important nuance, and one that cuts *against* over-claiming: the rate rise did happen, and it does push the date out.

## The structural facts that don't depend on any projection

These are the durable ones, safe to use:

- **Sensitivity:** the minimum annual asset return that would avoid a negative reserve within 50 years is **12.9%** — *"considerably higher than the best-estimate assumption."* Even excellent investment performance does not solve this. Return sensitivity moves exhaustion by only **±1 year** (2035 at 5.25% yield → 2037 at 6.25%).
- **Migration** barely moves it: a large positive net-migration scenario cuts GAP 25.5→24.8 and leaves the **year of exhaustion unaffected**.
- **From the 9th review (AR2013):** contributor-to-pensioner ratio falls from **3.7 : 1 → 1.1 : 1** over 50 years; pensioners rise from 135,049 to 353,255. **This is demographic, not political** — it does not depend on any policy assumption.

**The point:** NIS's funding problem cannot be invested out of, migrated out of, or grown out of. Only contributions up or benefits down. Both have happened, and the actuary says more is needed.

## What the 9th review recommended, and what happened

The 9th review (AR2013, published June 2015) is a useful control — **its recommendations were largely implemented**, which is evidence the actuary's advice does eventually land:

| 9th review recommendation | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Raise MIE $12,000 → **$13,600** | ✅ implemented (current) |
| Contribution rate → **13.2% in 2016** | ✅ implemented |
| **Maintain minimum pension at $3,000** | ✅ still $3,000 (2012 → today, **14 years unindexed**) |
| Retirement age **60 → 65 over 2025–2060** | ✅ implemented, **faster** (2028–2036) |
| Auto-adjust MIE, minimum pension, rate | ❌ not implemented |

**The minimum pension has been $3,000 since February 2012 and the 9th review explicitly recommended holding it there.** Fourteen years of inflation, unindexed — and it is the binding constraint for most retirees ([every basic rate is below it](nibtt-authoritative-rates.md)).

## How this lands in the product — carefully

The engine's retirement journey now has a chain of **five verified public facts**, each from the issuing agency:

1. **Whole-life averaging** — your pension reflects your *whole career*, not your final salary *(NIBTT training)*.
2. **$13,600/month ceiling** — earn above it, contribute, receive nothing extra *(NIBTT)*.
3. **Every basic rate is below the $3,000 minimum** — a top earner needs ~20 years of contributions just to beat the floor a minimum earner gets *(NIBTT benefit table)*.
4. **$3,000 minimum unindexed since 2012** *(NIBTT + 9th review)*.
5. **The actuary's own numbers** — expenditure has exceeded contributions since 2016–17; GAP 25.5% vs a rate reaching 19.2% in 2027; the 2019 recommendation arrived in 2026, and the actuary said it wasn't enough anyway *(10th review)*.

**None of this is a sales pitch. It is the government's published position about its own system.** That is precisely what makes it usable — and what makes overstating it unnecessary and self-defeating.

### Rules for using this in client-facing output
- ✅ **"The most recent published actuarial review projects…"** — always attributed, always dated.
- ✅ Prefer the **structural** facts (ceiling, minimum, averaging, demographics) over the **projections** — they don't decay.
- ❌ **Never** "NIS will run out in 2035." It's a 2016-vintage projection under superseded assumptions.
- ❌ Never present the exhaustion date without noting the reforms since.
- ⚖️ The honest framing: **"NIS is a floor, not a plan."** Everything above supports that and nothing requires exaggeration.
