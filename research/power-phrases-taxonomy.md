## Why this is worth keeping — and why the phrases themselves mostly aren't

**The transferable asset is the rhetorical STRUCTURE, not the wording.** Much of the wording is 1950s–80s vintage and, used verbatim in T&T in 2026, would range from dated to actively wrong (see Traps below). The structures are timeless.

### The six structures

**1. The tag question** — *"…isn't it?" / "…aren't they?" / "…don't they?"*
Roughly a third of the list ends this way. It is a deliberate device: it **invites agreement rather than asserting a claim**, converting a statement into something the client completes themselves.
> #5 *"More good things are lost by indecision than by wrong decision, aren't they?"*
> #33 *"No debt should last longer than the person who created it, should it?"*

**2. The reversal** — flip the objection's subject from the client to the family.
> #3 *"You can say 'I don't need it', but can you say 'my family won't need it'?"*
> #16 *"If you wouldn't like to live the rest of your life on the face amount of your present insurance, how do you expect your wife to?"*

**3. The reframe** — recast a cost as a choice already being made.
> #6 *"Someone always pays for life insurance, whether a man buys or not; the question is, who — the man or his family?"*
> #34 *"If you buy insurance or not, in the end someone will pay for it."*

**4. The analogy** — make the abstract physical. *This is Feldman's signature and the strongest device in the list.*
> #14 spare tyre / umbrella / parachute · #41 *"insure the eggs or the goose that laid them?"* · #99 the **three-legged stool** (death, early retirement, long-term disability) · #115–116 the **money machine** · #60 *"Life insurance is like a parachute… if you ever want it and have not got it, you will never need it again."*

**5. The contrast pair** — two states, one line.
> #9 *"Wives may not believe in life insurance, but widows always do."*
> #8 *"The difference between an old man and an elderly gentleman can be a decent income."*
> #22 *"It isn't easy to be old or poor, but it's a great deal worse to be both."*

**6. The cost-of-inaction frame** — price the decision, not the product.
> #65 *"Would you rather make an $800 mistake or a $250,000 mistake?"*
> #44 *"A lot of people do nothing wrong; they just do nothing. That's what's wrong."*

## 🔴 TRAPS — phrases that are wrong or harmful in T&T

**Using these naively would embarrass the agent or contradict our own design.**

### 1. Tax framings that don't exist in T&T
- **#37** *"Have you a plan to pay for capital gains tax or will you let the family sell assets to cover your oversight?"* → **T&T has no capital gains tax on most assets.**
- **#58** *"Would your executor be embarrassed by the lack of liquidity in your estate?"* and the estate-liquidity theme generally → **T&T has NO estate, inheritance, or gift tax.** The entire US/Australian estate-liquidity pitch is inapplicable.
- **#59** *"estate equalisation"* → still valid, but for a **different reason**: probate speed and asset titling, not tax. Life insurance proceeds **bypass probate** — *that* is the T&T estate pitch.
- **#106** *"superannuation"* → Australian. T&T is **NIS + approved annuity**.
- **#123** dollar's future value → fine, but T&T-specific inflation and the **$3,000 NIS minimum unindexed since 2012** are stronger and verifiable.

**This is the same localization failure the whole project is built to avoid** — importing a US/AU planning assumption that doesn't hold here. The estate-tax driver was already stripped from the `NeedsEngine` for exactly this reason.

### 2. The gendered framing directly contradicts our household model

A large fraction assume **male breadwinner, female homemaker, nuclear family**:
> #2 *"a home without a mother… a mother without a home"* · #9 *"Wives may not believe… widows always do"* · #15/#61 *"the worst time for a wife to become a breadwinner"* · #31 *"My wife does not need insurance as she is a housewife"* (Tolani #31 too) · #62 *"One ordinary father can support four children"* · #64 *"If every wife knew what every widow knows, every husband would be insured for more"*

**Our household canvas explicitly rejects this.** We designed for elderly parents, outside children, supported nephews, relatives overseas — *"it doesn't have to be official."* That was the whole point of idea #14, and it's the thing no US tool models.

**Using "wives and widows" language would undercut our single most differentiated design decision.** #92 shows the better register — *"your partner"* — and #73 is better still: *"If you weren't here, who would you want to take care of your obligations?"* — obligor-neutral, and it's exactly what the canvas asks.

**Rule for the explanation layer: keep the structure, replace the cast.** *"Wives may not believe in life insurance, but widows always do"* → the reversal survives; the gendering doesn't.

### 3. Manipulative edges
- **#19** *"You can put me off, but you can't put my competitors off — death and disability."*
- **#24** *"Whatever reason you may have for not starting this plan now will only sound ridiculous to your widow."*
- **#43** *"Anybody who doesn't believe in it ought to have the right to die without it at least once."*

These are pressure, not information. They sit badly against the **Market Conduct Guideline**'s suitability duties and against a product whose entire pitch is *"we don't optimise for commission."* **Exclude.**

## How this enters the product

**It does not enter the engine. It enters the `AIExplanationService` and the fact-find's soft questions, as a register — nothing more.**

Concretely:
1. **#45 becomes a fact-find soft question** — *"What formula did you use to arrive at the cover you have now?"* It's the X-ray opener and it's honest: it doesn't assert a gap, it reveals one.
2. **#125 becomes the trust opener** — *"What's been your problem with my industry in the past?"* In T&T that surfaces CLICO on the client's terms, before it becomes silent resistance.
3. **The analogies (#41 goose/eggs, #99 three-legged stool, #115 money machine) are keepers** — they make the abstract concrete, which is exactly what the leak and the obligation counter already do visually.
4. **The tag-question device** matches our fact-find tone (*"it doesn't have to be official"* is the same move — it invites rather than asserts).
5. **Everything gendered gets recast obligor-neutral.** Everything estate-tax gets deleted. Everything coercive gets excluded.

**The test, unchanged:** *does it help explain a recommendation the engine already made on the merits — or is it doing the recommending?* Only the first is allowed.
