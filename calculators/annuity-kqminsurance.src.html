An honest answer

A registered annuity would not cut your tax right now

Why the tax break does nothing for you
The whole point of a registered annuity is that the money you put in comes off your taxable income. If you are not paying income tax, there is nothing for it to come off. The deduction would be worth exactly $0 to you.
What would change it
Saving is still worth doing
None of this means don't save — it means a registered annuity is the wrong wrapper for you today. An unregistered plan does the same job without tying the money to a maturity date between 50 and 70, and without the penalty for reaching it early. You give up a tax break you cannot use anyway.

The catch worth knowing. A company-funded plan is owned by the employer, so it fails the “purchased by an individual” test for the Finance Act 2026 exemption — the income is not tax-free at maturity, unlike a personal registered plan. You get a much larger deduction going in and a worse tax position coming out. Which wins depends on the numbers and on how long the money has to grow.

And when it pays out, it is tax-free

Since 1 January 2026 the income from an approved deferred annuity is exempt from income tax when it matures. Relief going in, no tax coming out — but the exemption has conditions worth knowing before you sign anything.

You buy it yourself
Purchased by, and payable to, a resident of Trinidad & Tobago. A plan your employer owns does not qualify.
It matures between 50 and 70
Outside that window the payout is legal but loses the exemption.
You keep it to maturity
Cash it in early and the whole sum is taxable.
Plans you already have count
Approved before, on or after 1 January 2026 — an existing policy qualifies.
Finance Act 2026, passed 10 June 2026, effective 1 January 2026, amending the Income Tax Act Chap. 75:01.
